Localization for a multilingual support assistant

What changes when native reviewers adapt support content for each locale instead of translating it word for word.

Illustrative case study written to show structure — not a description of a real client engagement.

The challenge

A support assistant that is translated rather than localized reads as foreign: the intent shifts, the tone is too stiff or too casual, and edge cases such as formal address or regional wording are missed. The team needs content that works for each locale, with a way to show it was checked.

The approach

  • Glossary: Product terms and fixed phrases are agreed once per locale and shared with every reviewer.
  • Native review: Reviewers who live the locale read each item for meaning, fluency and regional fit.
  • Intent: Each phrase is checked against the intent behind it, so a reply still does what the source reply does.
  • Tone: A short locale brief sets register and formality; reviewers flag any drift from it.
  • Edge cases: Dialect words, mixed-language input, numbers, dates and polite forms are collected as a running list.
  • LQA: A linguistic quality pass scores a sample with a written error taxonomy, and corrections go back into the glossary.

The outcome

What the team receives: localized content per locale, the glossary and tone brief behind it, an issue log by category and a delivery report. No improvement figure or percentage is shown here; any such claim needs client evidence.

Takeaways

  • Separate translation from adaptation in the plan.
  • Write the tone brief before the first item.
  • Evaluate each locale with native reviewers.
