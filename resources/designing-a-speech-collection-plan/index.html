Designing a speech collection plan

Who speaks, what they say, where they record and how you will know the data is balanced: a planning checklist for speech data.

Describe the target population

Start from the people who will use the product: languages, dialects, age bands, genders, accents and devices. Turn that description into quotas. A plan without quotas tends to over-represent whoever is easiest to recruit.

Choose the speech style

Read speech gives control and phonetic coverage; spontaneous speech gives realism. Many plans mix both: scripted prompts for coverage, guided conversations for natural turn-taking, hesitations and code-switching.

Specify the acoustic conditions

List the environments that matter (quiet room, home, car, street, call line), the devices and the microphone distance. Log these as structured metadata per file so you can filter and balance later.

Settle consent and privacy early

Decide what contributors agree to, how long recordings are kept, whether personal details may appear in speech and how they are removed. Write it down before the first session.

Plan quality checks

Define audio checks (clipping, silence, sample rate), content checks (did they say the prompt?) and transcript checks. Track quota fill live so gaps close during collection, not after.
